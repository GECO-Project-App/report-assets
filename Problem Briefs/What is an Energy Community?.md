# What is an Energy Community?

Introduction

Promoting Energy Involvement for Sustainable Rural Development

•	In Gotland's rural communities, the concept of energy communities remains largely unknown, especially among older populations. This unfamiliarity is a critical barrier to adopting and successfully implementing energy communities, as it hampers the transition towards community-based sustainable energy practices. The main issue observed is that older participants rarely mention the concept of energy communities in interviews, suggesting a possible lack of awareness or understanding of the concept itself. This gap significantly limits participation and undermines the initiatives' potential to contribute to environmental sustainability, economic resilience, and social cohesion.

•	The primary objective is to bridge this knowledge gap, ensuring that all residents, particularly the older demographic, understand the benefits and workings of energy communities. Educating the population is crucial for fostering active participation and support for these initiatives, thereby promoting a more sustainable, self-sufficient, and resilient model of rural development in line with Sweden's environmental and sustainability goals.

Empathy-Driven Problem Statement

•	This gap in understanding and engagement isn't just a matter of adopting new technologies or practices; it's about securing a future where sustainable living and community cohesion are intertwined. Elders find themselves on the fringes of this transformative movement, not due to a lack of interest, but from a lack of accessible, relatable information. This oversight not only diminishes the potential for a collective shift towards environmental sustainability but also risks leaving behind a valuable segment of our community whose involvement could enrich these initiatives with their experience and insight.

•	Our challenge, then, is to bridge this gap with empathy and inclusivity at the forefront. We must craft approaches that resonate on a personal level, making the concept of energy communities not just understandable but meaningful to our older population. This is not merely about promoting energy efficiency or the concept of the energy community; it's about weaving the fabric of a community where every member, regardless of age, feels valued, heard, and integral to our shared sustainable future. It's a call to ensure that in the transition towards greener practices, we do not inadvertently forge a divide but instead build bridges that span generations, fostering a truly inclusive movement towards sustainability.

	
Target Audience / Stakeholders

•	The target audience for this project comprises older members of rural communities on Gotland, characterized by a blend of traditional values and open-mindedness towards new concepts, albeit with varying degrees of familiarity with technology. These individuals are predominantly retirees who value the democratic principles their communities stand on, emphasizing collective decision-making and active participation in community life. Their retirement provides them with the time to engage deeply in communal activities, fostering a strong sense of belonging and contribution.

•	This TA enjoys the social aspect of their communities, often gathering together for events, discussions, and projects that enhance the collective well-being of their surroundings. While they may not always be versed in the latest technological advancements, their openness to new ideas presents an opportunity to introduce and integrate the concept of energy communities in a way that resonates with their values and lifestyle. Their active involvement and influence within their communities make them essential stakeholders in the transition towards sustainable living practices, leveraging their collective experience, wisdom, and available time to contribute meaningfully to environmental stewardship and community resilience.
